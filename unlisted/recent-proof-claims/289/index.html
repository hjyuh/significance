01 · Scope

What is the result?

The strengthened statement gives the construction’s scope; the original problem is a special case.

Claim in briefFor every positive rational, lower bound, and separation, the paper claims representations using exactly k intervals of length two or three for every sufficiently large k.
Scope boundaryThe paper also claims a denominator bound and many distinct representations. The threshold is described as extremely large; this is an existence theorem, not a practical small-k construction.

02 · Proof route

Follow the construction

Each stage must preserve the reciprocal sum while managing the interval count, denominator, and spacing.

1

Cancel the leading prime-power residue

Use a prime Q and correction pairs to reduce the largest prime-power obstruction in the denominator.

Paper §§3–5 · main analytic bottleneck: count coprime factorizations Qm + 1 = ab.
2

Repeat a finite descent

Apply residue-response results to remove successive levels, while bounding the total correction mass and ensuring strict descent.

Paper §5 · check uniformity and the total loss estimate.
3

Complete the small remainder

Reserved pairs absorb the remaining rational exactly without changing the required component count.

Paper §6 · verify the completion lemma and spacing constraints.
4

Switch cells to tune the count

A local identity trades five triples for six pairs, changing the number of intervals by one without changing the sum.

Paper §7 · audit all residue bands and cross-family separation.
5

Supply enough switches at every scale

Powersmooth cells give many independent switches; overlap between consecutive scales then covers all sufficiently large counts.

Paper §§8–9 · highest follow-through checks: sieve count, prefix estimate, and overlap deduction.

03 · Earlier claims

Same problem, three different proof engines

The claim summaries share an exact-count construction using intervals of size two or three. They differ in stated scope, organizing ideas, and formal evidence.

Yuren Tang · 7 Sep 2026

Residue structure and a global transversal

Tang organizes the construction through fibres and an lcm torsion filtration. Fixed-cardinality residue responses use the Dias da Silva–Hamidoune theorem; reciprocal-neutral switches vary the interval count, and prime stages create enough count width. Haxell’s independent-transversal theorem makes the interval choices simultaneously disjoint and nonadjacent.

The claim includes an exact-arithmetic verifier for Appendix C and equation (3.1); that is not an end-to-end Lean formalization.

Johan Land · 4 Sep 2026

Prime-pair absorption and deficit repair

Land’s summary builds suitable pairs using a Bourgain–Garaev estimate, uses sparse inverse coverage to cancel denominator prime powers, and applies Liu–Sawhney pair-to-triple extensions to absorb the remaining count deficit. It states an explicit interval range up to 20k and enforces nonadjacency.

The submission said formalization was forthcoming; this audit has not checked the current state of the linked repository.

David Budden, using PingYou · 26 Sep 2026

Analytic residue cancellation and count switches

PingYou’s summary advertises arbitrary positive rational targets, arbitrary lower bounds and separations, and at least 2k/21 distinct representations. Its route uses exponential-sum factorization estimates, prime-power residue descent, reserved pairs, a local five-triples/six-pairs switch, and powersmooth cells to cover all large counts.

The claim says “Lean to follow”; Appendix A computations do not formalize the proof.
