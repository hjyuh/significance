01 · Scope

What is and isn’t claimed

“Partial” describes the broader problem, not whether the paper proves its specific subclaim.

Claim in briefFor every sufficiently large k, there is an n < Lk such that every prime divisor of binom(n, k) exceeds k. Equivalently, g(k) < Lk.
Still open in scopeThis does not give the sharp order log g(k) ≍ k / log k or resolve the successive-ratio conjectures.

02 · Proof route

From a modified lcm to a witness

The argument turns divisibility into a sieve for one multiplier.

1

Modify the lcm

Choose a modulus M close enough to Lk to leave a long interval of possible multipliers.

Paper §2 · control log(M/Lk) and the available range for t.
2

Translate prime divisibility into forbidden residues

Lucas’s digit criterion handles small primes and yields residue conditions in three prime ranges.

Paper §3 · check middle-prime p² lifting and the top-band count.
3

Budget the forbidden density

Prime number theorem estimates bound the total density of multipliers that fail any prime condition.

Paper §3 · fixed proportional intervals and the three-term theta identity.
4

Count survivors in a finite interval

Truncated inclusion–exclusion estimates survivors; a CRT discrepancy term accounts for counting in the actual finite interval.

Paper §§4–5 · highest-risk review: truncated CRT intersections and Bonferroni tail constants.
5

Convert the survivor into g(k) < Lk

A surviving multiplier gives n = Mt − 1; the exact inequalities and least-element definition finish the deduction.

Paper §5 · statement correspondence checks strictness and the uniform threshold.
