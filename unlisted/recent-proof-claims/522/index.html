Turn random roots into a deterministic slope.

The claim concerns the almost-sure number of roots in the unit disk for a sequence of random polynomials with independent fair signs. The paper proves a stronger radial law.

01 · Scope

One theorem, then separate extensions

Do not make the reader guess which part solves the Erdős question.

Main claimed resultFor one infinite sequence of independent fair signs, the root count in the closed unit disk, divided by n, tends almost surely to 1/2, with roots counted with multiplicity.
Scope boundariesThe paper’s stronger radial profile has the claim as x = 0. Other coefficient laws and the constant-128 log-rate bound are separate extensions. The (0/1) interpretation is a distinct open variant.

02 · Proof route

Five steps from Jensen to all degrees

The essential difficulty is extending a probabilistic estimate on selected degrees to every degree almost surely.

1

Zeros become slopes

Jensen’s formula expresses zero counts through secant slopes of the angular mean of (log|f_n|).

Paper §§2–3 · connect exact normalization and radius conventions.
2

Predict the mean profile

A deterministic variance profile predicts the limiting radial slope; at the unit circle it gives 1/2.

Paper §§2–4 · check use of variance and log-moment bounds.
3

Concentrate on sparse degrees

Small-ball/occupation estimates and a logarithmic-moment input yield concentration; Borel–Cantelli gives the law along (N=j^8).

Paper §§4–5 · Nazarov–Nishry–Sodin is the deepest external input.
4

Match roots between selected degrees

Bound appended tails, discard roots with small derivatives, then use Taylor estimates and Rouché’s theorem to track regular roots to intermediate degrees.

Paper §6 · check exceptional-root and root-crossing counts.
5

Finish the almost-sure limit

Control degree differences and use monotonicity to upgrade fixed-radius results to compact-uniform radial convergence.

Paper §6 · separate the main theorem from further profile refinements.
