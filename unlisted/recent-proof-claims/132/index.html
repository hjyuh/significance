01 · Result cards

Keep the two theorem paths apart

One is a restricted case of the original problem. The other is a constant-factor result for a related asymptotic question.

Formalized result A

Convex-layer bound for the second distance

Under the local condition that every hull point has at most three second-layer neighbors at distance Δ₂, plus a size condition on L₁, the formal theorem gives multiplicity at most n. With the Hopf–Pannwitz diameter result, it yields two rare distances for this restricted family.

Does not prove the condition from the paper’s “no four points concyclic” phrasing.

Formalized result B

A 4/3 bound for CDL Problem 1.6

The formal theorem bounds min{μ(Δ₂), μ(δ)} by (4/3)n + C. Its Lean constant is 10¹⁶; the paper displays an analytic constant near 4.73 × 10⁹.

Related asymptotic problem; does not settle #132’s two-distances-at-most-n assertion.

Wingate Jones and Evan Beller: related, different scopes

Wingate Jones · 25 Sep 2026

Conditional layer bound, plus a separate asymptotic bound

One result handles a family of point sets under a convex-layer hypothesis: at most three second-layer neighbors per hull point, together with a bound on the first-layer size. The paper’s “no four points concyclic” condition still needs a formal bridge to that hypothesis.

A second result proves a 4/3 constant-factor estimate for Clemen–Dumitrescu–Liu Problem 1.6. That is a related asymptotic question, not the general assertion in #132.

Lean reports the named layer theorem and 4/3 result; the paper’s sharper constant and several other results are outside that exact formal scope.

Evan Beller · 23 Aug 2026

A finite case: the first assertion when n = 8

Pair counting forces the distance multiplicities to be (9, 9, 9, 1). Removing either endpoint of the unique diametral pair leaves a seven-point three-distance set. A classification of those sets, followed by a six-point overlap-rigidity argument, yields a contradiction.

The Lean deduction is conditional on three literature inputs: the Hopf–Pannwitz diameter bound, a lower bound of four distances for eight planar points, and the classification of seven-point three-distance sets.

This settles one finite case only. It does not establish the general claim as n grows.

Evan’s claim account · pinned Lean repository

How they fit togetherBeller’s result is a sharply scoped finite-case proof with explicit external dependencies. Jones’s first result aims at a broader conditional family; his second gives a constant-factor bound for a different problem. The finite case can serve as a comparison point, but the two proof routes do not subsume one another.
