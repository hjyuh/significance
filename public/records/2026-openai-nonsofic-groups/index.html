What this result says — editor — digest / significance-editor

A group here is a set of reversible operations you can undo and combine. Soficity asks whether an infinite group can be imitated, to any accuracy you like, by shuffling a finite deck of cards: every product you can form in the group is matched by a shuffle that agrees almost everywhere. Most groups anyone writes down can be imitated this way, and for decades nobody produced one that provably cannot. The manuscript names a candidate: the reversible elements of a small algebra built from two symbols. Its claim is that no finite shuffle imitates this group well enough. What Significance holds is the artifacts around that claim -- the manuscript file, the formal code, the logs of rebuilding it -- not a reading of the argument itself.
